How should autoencoders compress for interactive world models?

Anonymous authors, under review for ICLR 2027

Drag the divider on any video to compare the real future with the generated one.

Video world models generate in the latent space of an autoencoder. Its compression sets the cost of every frame and how long each action waits to take effect. We built CADRE, an open family of causal diffusion representation autoencoders that covers seven spatio-temporal compressions, and trained the same world model on each in four environments.

Below are rollouts in the order of the paper. Each one starts from 2 s of real video. The world model then generates 4.8 s from the recorded actions alone, at 20 frames per second. Blue marks generated video, ochre real video.

§2

A latent frame waits for all its actions

The autoencoder compresses a clip by τ in time and s in space. A latent frame covers τ video frames, so the world model can predict it only once all τ actions are known. The first action of each chunk waits τ/f, then one world-model call and one decode.

§5

Four environments, generated in real time

We use two manipulation environments with a still camera, a robot and human hands, and two navigation environments with a moving camera, driving and indoor. On one H100 the world models on CADRE generate 22 to 39 frames per second, more than the 20 they display. The first clip in each environment is the one in the paper's figure.

§5.1

A compressed latent needs a diffusion decoder

A strongly compressed latent fits many videos. MIRA's perceptual regression decoder returns their blurry average. CADRE's diffusion decoder samples one sharp video, in one or two steps thanks to self-distillation. Both compress by 2×32×32. Drag the divider to compare them.

Decode a compressed latent by diffusion. In one step it runs as fast as regression, with 1.4 to 2.5× lower FVD and similar action following. A second step helps further.
§5.2

Off-the-shelf autoencoders break when the camera moves

The world model is identical in every panel. Only the latent changes. With a moving camera, Wan 2.2 and LTX-2 dissolve into texture, Cosmos leaves ghosts and MIRA blurs. With a still camera most of them look alike. Under each panel are that autoencoder's FVD in this environment and its measured action latency.

App. I

One context, several futures

Each rollout starts from the same real context, then follows either the recorded actions or a sequence never recorded with that context: no motion, another clip's actions, or a constant left or right turn. The futures split when the context ends.

§6.1

Compress time in the autoencoder, not the world model

Both routes reach the same token grid. The autoencoder route is a CADRE member that packs τ frames into each latent. The world-model route keeps the per-frame latent of CADRE-1×16×16 and patches it by τ inside the world model. Switch on the error map to see where each rollout departs from the real robot.

Let the autoencoder compress in time. At the same token count its frames look sharper and move more realistically, with similar action following. It learns to reconstruct pixels, while the world model keeps only what helps it predict.
§6.2

Long latents are free with a still camera, and seam with a moving one

Every rollout here uses the same fine token, s = 16, and only the number of frames per latent changes. The plot tracks how much each generated frame differs from the previous one. The ticks mark chunk boundaries for the τ you point at. With a moving camera the change piles up on those boundaries as τ grows.

Manipulation and navigation call for opposite compressions. A still camera makes content redundant over time, so long latents are cheap. A moving camera brings new content every frame, so long latents seam, while coarse spatial tokens cost little.
§6.3

Coarser tokens keep frames sharp but lose the motion

Both rollouts pack the same number of frames per latent. The coarse one also compresses more in space. Each frame is just as sharp, but the arms drift from where the actions put them. FVD roughly doubles, while FID moves far less.

Coarser tokens hurt motion and action following far more than image quality. This is strongest in manipulation, where the action happens in a small region that stays in view.
App. G

Sixty-four seconds of robot manipulation

This uses the same protocol, run for 1280 generated frames. CADRE degrades smoothly. Wan 2.2 starts near the reconstruction regime, but its colours saturate after about half a minute. MIRA's blur grows into dark blobs. Under each panel is its FVD at 64 s.
